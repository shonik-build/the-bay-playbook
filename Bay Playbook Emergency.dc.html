Emergency contacts and consulate

Dial 911 from a cellphone in San Francisco and you might get the California Highway Patrol instead of SFPD. Fremont's police department says it on their own site: a 911 call from a cell outside the immediate area lands with CHP first, and CHP doesn't receive an address from cell callers. Usually the transfer takes a few seconds. Sometimes it doesn't.

The consulate has opened 2pm to 4pm on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays since June 2024. It isn't a general walk-in window. Those two hours cover three things and nothing else: an emergency visa, an Emergency Certificate for travel to India that same day, and mortal remains going out the same day. Call +1-415-483-6629 first to confirm you qualify and find out what to bring. Driving to SoMa on a Sunday to be told no is a bad afternoon.

There's also an Open House on working days, 9:30 to 11:30am, for enquiries and grievances, by appointment.

NOBODY TELLS YOU
The consulate has warned that fraudsters spoof its main number, 415-891-4080, to run scams. Seeing that number on your screen proves nothing. Hang up, call back.
One more thing that tells you a lot: the consulate's own Contact Us page still lists an old address on Arguello Boulevard while every other page on the same site says 71 Stevenson St. Nobody caught it. Check twice before you drive anywhere.

If you lose your passport

Step one gates everything else, so do it right.

{{ s.n }}
{{ s.t }}
{{ s.d }}

The VFS checklist is full of small trapdoors that will bounce your file. The envelope has to be superscribed "RE-ISSUANCE IN LIEU OF LOST PASSPORT". One envelope holds exactly one application. Prepaid FedEx labels aren't accepted. You need both an Indian and a US address on the form, and online registration before mailing is mandatory.

On timing, the consulate's own position is that you shouldn't even enquire until 20 working days have gone by. That's four weeks of business days as the floor, not the answer. A lost-passport re-issue also triggers police verification back in India, and that's the step that wrecks every estimate. Plan for six to eight weeks. Give VFS your correct Indian address with the right police station and district, because a sloppy address is the most common reason verification stalls for months.

A FRIEND WILL TELL YOU
The Emergency Certificate is built primarily for people without valid US status: an expired passport and no legal status, deportation orders, a lost passport with no valid visa. On a live O-1 or H-1B you are not the standard case, and the consular officer's decision is final either way. Call the emergency line and ask before you plan around it. An EC gets you to India; coming back needs a new passport and a new visa stamp.

Your visa stamp was inside that passport and it's gone with it. That's a conversation with an immigration attorney, not a webpage. Meanwhile: scan your passport, visa and I-94 into cloud storage, print one set, and leave it somewhere that isn't your bag. It turns a crisis into paperwork, which is the best outcome available.

Break-ins, landlords, crashes

Locked out, no heat, burst pipe: your landlord or the building's emergency line first, which is why you save it the day you sign the lease. SF tenants can escalate habitability complaints to 311.

Car break-in: photograph everything before you touch anything, then file. SFPD takes vehicle burglary, theft, vandalism and tampering online. Stolen vehicles, or anything with a known suspect, need a call or a station visit. Then the rule every long-timer repeats and every newcomer ignores exactly once: nothing visible in a parked car. Not a bag, not a jacket, not a charging cable.

Crash: get to safety, 911 if anyone is hurt, photos and insurance details. Don't discuss fault at the scene with anyone.

The earthquake setup

You'll feel one. Almost all of them are nothing. The setup takes four minutes and you do it once.

MyShake sends a ShakeAlert warning for magnitude 4.5 and up, a few seconds ahead of the shaking. Free, both platforms, built at UC Berkeley. Android already has ShakeAlert in the OS under Settings, then Safety and emergency, then Earthquake alerts. Turn it on either way. Drop, cover, hold on. You don't run outside.

Then sign up for your county's alerts. In San Francisco, text your ZIP to 888-777 or use alertsf.org. Alameda runs AC Alert, San Mateo has SMC Alert, Santa Clara has AlertSCC. Register more than one ZIP: where you sleep and where you work.

The part that contradicts everything above

All of this assumes you'll read a webpage during an emergency. You won't. You'll open your phone, your hands will shake, and you'll dial the one number you can remember.

So if you take one thing from this page, take the lock-screen card. Seven numbers on an image, ten minutes on a quiet Sunday. Everything else here, the fair pricing thresholds, the VFS envelope rules, who actually qualifies for an EC, is for the afternoon you spend cleaning up afterwards. It'll still be here.
