Networking in SF

Where the events live, which rooms are worth paying for, and how to run all of it on a calendar that still has a team in IST attached to it.

Any given week runs more founder galas, hackathons, demo nights and late-night builders' cafes than you could attend if attending were your job, and half the good ones are waitlisted before you hear about them. The density is why people rave and why people quit. It is also how a calendar becomes a badge collection, which is the one thing your first 90 days should not produce.

Both are right. Pick two or three, go deep, and the only real question is which.

Luma is the rail, and the good stuff is unlisted

Most SF events never surface on Luma's public discover page. By some counts the large majority are unlisted and visible only if you follow the host's calendar. So don't browse. Subscribe to ten or fifteen host calendars and let the events come to you.

Partiful is the second layer, house parties and socials rather than talks, and it gates hard on a US phone number, so sort your eSIM first. Which makes a cheap eSIM and a filled-out LinkedIn part of your networking stack, oddly.

The permanent circuit of ticketed mega-mixers fails every part of that test, so skip it. One or two curated events a week beats five random ones. You're after repeat encounters with the same people, not contact volume. Where an event asks for your LinkedIn or a short application, fill it out properly. The host is curating the room and a blank profile gets rejected. Apply the day a listing posts, not the day before it happens, because half the good ones waitlist.

THE OTHER HALF OF THE GRAPH
Group chats. The WhatsApp and Discord threads where hosts drop events before they're listed and intros get traded in real time. There is no application form. You get added by someone who has seen you twice. That's the repeat-encounters point becoming actual infrastructure. Once you have the context, ask outright. "Is there a group where this crew coordinates?" is a normal question here, not a presumptuous one.

The annual anchor is SF Tech Week each October, run by a16z. The public calendar is maybe half of what actually happens that week. The value is in the unlisted side dinners, and you get into those by asking the hosts of the small events you did attend.

Hackathons

Weekend builds nearly every week, AI-heavy, with Cerebral Valley and AI Tinkerers as the two rails to watch. 48 hours of building next to someone tells you more than six months of coffee, so demo something even if it's rough. The demo is the networking.

Coffee walks

The default first one-to-one is not a conference room. It's a 30 to 45 minute walk with coffee, and you suggest it explicitly. "Want to do a coffee walk near the Mission?" Side by side is candid in a way across-the-table never is.

Three or four a week is the ceiling if you're also running a company, not the target. Two or three, each ending with a concrete next step or an intro you owe, compounds fine.

Dinners, and the rung below hosting

An eight-person curated dinner produces more real relationships than a 200-person mixer. Hosting one is the strongest move a newcomer can make, and you don't need to feel established to do it.

There's a rung below hosting that costs nothing. Most SF events run understaffed, so message the organiser on the Luma page and offer to work check-in or take photos. You've just gone from attendee to team, with a reason to talk to everyone who walks in. Thank the organisers on your way out regardless. They're the best-connected and least-thanked people in the room, and helpers get invited back, sometimes onto the stage.

When you do host, steal one serial host's trick. Go around the table and have each person introduce the person to their left, not themselves. Everyone has to have a real conversation before dinner starts and nobody gets to recite their pitch. Venues, budgets and the sponsor model sit on the Where to Host Events page.

Recurring rooms, and the two with doors

Frequency is the whole mechanism, so a weekly community builds trust far faster than a monthly one. Choosing between two groups, pick the one that meets weekly. The desi founder communities are mapped with entry paths in the Desi Directory. Pick one anchor there, then make sure at least half your meetings by month three happen outside it, for the reasons on the culture page.

A lot of it happens in workout clothes, with the pitch pressure removed. Founders Running Club meets Saturdays in the Panhandle, casual pace groups, coffee after. Show up three weeks in a row and you have a network. The weekends page has the rest of the circuit.

"I just moved to SF" is the strongest cold-outreach line you get, and it expires around week 8. Use it in month one on the people you'd otherwise be too intimidated to message.

The two rules that govern everything

Don't pitch first
The give-first norm is real. Lead with curiosity or a useful offer and let the other person ask what you're building. Pitching in the first five minutes marks you as a taker. The full translation of this, and how to read the polite American no, is on the culture page.
Follow up inside 24 hours
While they still remember you. Reference something specific from the conversation and include something useful, a link or an intro offer. Never a generic "great to meet you." Past the first dozen people memory stops scaling. A plain spreadsheet of who, where, and what you owe them beats every networking app.

If you're an operator, not a founder

"What are you building?" still opens every conversation, so have an answer about your craft or the problem you own. "I work at X" ends conversations here in a way it doesn't in Bangalore. Hiring runs through the dinner-and-group-chat graph long before it reaches a listing, and the person who pulls you into your next role will be someone who has watched you think out loud three times. If you're technical, shipping something over a weekend is the fastest way to become legible to the founders who are hiring.

THE COUNTER-POSITION, WHICH SERIOUS BUILDERS HOLD
That the circuit is mostly hype, and that New York founders network half the week while SF founders disappear and ship. They aren't wrong. This page is built to survive that critique. One or two events a week, protected shipping time, and the demo as the best networking there is.
One caveat. The venue layer churns fast, and today's centre of gravity is usually a building nobody was talking about two years ago. Trust the channels on this page longer than the addresses, and check membership prices on the room's own site rather than here.
