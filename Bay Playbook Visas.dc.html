Visas for Indian founders

Pick the right path before you book flights. Most Indian founders land on one of four: a B-1 to scout, an O-1A to build, an L-1A if you already run a real Indian company, or an H-1B if you already hold one.

B-1: scout the market, don't work

Meetings, contract negotiation, investor conversations, conferences. Not: production code for your US entity, US salary, or "running" US ops day-to-day. Border officers have tightened since 2025, so carry the return ticket and the meeting schedule.

THE REAL BOTTLENECK IS THE INTERVIEW QUEUE
First-time B1/B2 interview waits at Indian consulates ran roughly 100 to 400 days across 2025 and 2026, city-by-city. Chennai and Hyderabad have consistently run shorter than Mumbai and Delhi, sometimes by months. If you already hold a valid B1/B2 from a past trip, you have dodged this entirely.

THE CONSULATE-CITY ARBITRAGE
Founders routinely fly to Chennai for an interview rather than wait 9+ months in Mumbai. One MRV fee lets you reschedule endlessly while hunting cancellation slots.
TELL THE OFFICER THE TRUTH, NARROWLY
You're visiting for meetings and conferences. Not "moving to SF." Carry a return ticket, a printed meeting schedule, and proof of ties to India.

O-1A: the founder default

"Extraordinary ability" sounds intimidating — in practice it's an evidence-assembly exercise. You need to hit 3 of 8 criteria codified at 8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii), unless you hold one major internationally-recognised award (Nobel, Olympic medal, etc — almost nobody does).

THE REQUIREMENT NOBODY PLANS FOR

Duration: up to 3 years initially, extensions in 1-year increments with no overall cap. Spouses get O-3 with no work authorisation, so plan for that.

L-1A: if you already run a company in India

If your Indian company is real and staffed, you can transfer yourself into a US entity instead of proving you are extraordinary. The test is structural rather than reputational: a qualifying parent/subsidiary/affiliate relationship, plus one continuous year of managerial or executive work at the Indian entity in the three years before filing. Evidence is payroll, org charts and contracts — not press clippings.

THE NEW OFFICE RULE
If the US entity is newly formed, USCIS approves you for one year, not three, and you spend that year proving you built something real. Officers also expect a secured physical premises — signed commercial lease with room for the headcount in your business plan. Virtual offices and co-working desks draw heavy scrutiny. Extension is judged on execution, not the plan you filed.
Timelines are shorter than the O-1A (about a month with premium processing) because the evidence already exists in your company records. Costs sit close: I-129 L is $1,385 or $695 for small employers, plus the $600/$300 asylum fee and a $500 fraud-prevention fee on new L-1 petitions. Premium processing is the same $2,965. Approval rates run a little lower and RFE rates higher than the O-1A, because new-office cases get read hard.

H-1B: the founder rule changed in 2025

If you already hold an H-1B, your Delaware C-corp can file a transfer with no new lottery. The bigger change is for owners. Since 17 January 2025, USCIS has removed the old employer-employee control test and expressly allows a founder with a controlling interest to be sponsored by their own company — provided you spend the majority of your time on specialty-occupation work rather than running the business. The trade-off is validity: initial approval and first extension are capped at 18 months each, against three years for a standard H-1B. You no longer need outside directors to make this work, which was true before 2025 and is repeated in a lot of stale advice.

Think green card from day one

The O-1A is temporary. The long-term play is EB-1A, the green card cousin with a higher evidence bar. EB-1 India is badly backlogged — the September 2026 Final Action Date sits around 15 October 2022, and realistic I-140-to-green-card timelines for India run 4 to 6 years.

File the I-140 as early as your evidence supports it.
Filing locks your priority date — your place in the queue. Every month you delay is a month added at the back end. If you came in on L-1A, your green-card cousin is EB-1C, not EB-1A: multinational manager or executive rather than extraordinary ability. Same backlogged queue, same filing-early logic.
Ask your lawyer about EB-1A timing in the same engagement as the O-1 — the priority date compounds, and adding it later costs six months you did not need to lose.
