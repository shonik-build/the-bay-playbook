Before you fly: remote setup

Four things get harder the moment you board: your Indian mobile number, your resident bank account, your driving paperwork, and the money you plan to move. Doing any of them from a sublet in Sunnyvale means a notarised affidavit, a consulate appointment, or a courier to a branch in Bangalore.

Everything else waits. Apps, groceries, a US credit card, the DMV. Trying to pre-solve those from India is wasted effort, and a couple of items on the standard before-you-fly list are better done late. The eSIM is one of them.

Keep the Indian number alive

Every Indian bank, UPI app, mutual fund, DigiLocker and Aadhaar service authenticates you with an SMS. Lose the number and you lose access to your Indian financial life, and getting it back from California is a months-long problem routed through your bank's overseas branch.

Two separate risks, and people conflate them.

RISK 1 · ROAMING
A SIM in a drawer in India receives nothing. You need international roaming active for the number to attach to a US network at all. Do it before you fly. On some plans the activation itself triggers an OTP, and that's a loop you can't break from a US IP address.
RISK 2 · DEACTIVATION
Under TRAI's rules a prepaid connection survives 90 days of non-usage while it holds ₹20 or more. Past 90 days, ₹20 buys another 30. Then 15 days of grace. Then the number is recycled, and every institution that authenticates you by SMS is authenticating someone else. Incoming calls and SMS count as usage.
NOBODY TELLS YOU
Convert to postpaid before flying if you're staying past six months. It costs more monthly, removes the recycling risk entirely, and postpaid roaming packs carry 365-day validity from purchase where prepaid packs expire in 30.

On the pack itself, most people over-buy. You're not browsing on Indian roaming at ₹45 a minute. You're receiving OTPs.

Your US number: buy it late

The standard advice is to buy a US eSIM four weeks out so you land with data. Half right. Buy it early and install the profile early, but check when billing starts. On Tello the 30 days begin as soon as the order processes, not when you activate in San Francisco. Order three weeks out and you've paid for three weeks of service in a country you weren't in. Buy in the last few days, install in India, switch on after landing.

Tell your bank you're leaving

This costs the most to skip and appears on almost no checklist. Under FEMA you cannot hold a resident savings account once your status changes to NRI. It isn't triggered by a day count alone. Leaving for business or an uncertain period establishes intent, which for a founder means the day you land. Running a resident account after that surfaces later as a frozen account or a KYC block, usually at the worst moment.

You get it back. TCS is an advance credit: it shows in your Form 26AS and offsets your liability when you file. But "back" means after your return is processed, which is a year of runway sitting with the tax department.

  • The threshold resets on 1 April. Flying between January and March, split the transfer across the boundary and you get two ₹10 lakh windows.
  • Quote your PAN on every transaction and check the PAN–Aadhaar link is live. Unlinked, the rate doubles.
  • Spending abroad on an international credit card sits outside LRS, so it carries no TCS. That's a standing deferral rather than a repeal, so confirm it still holds before you build a plan around it.
Not tax advice. Above roughly ₹50 lakh, or if you'll be RNOR in your first year, the split-year residency question is worth an hour with someone who does this professionally.

A US address that isn't your friend's couch

Mercury, Brex and Stripe all want a US mailing address before onboarding finishes, and your first debit card needs somewhere to go. A virtual mailbox solves it for $10–30 a month.

One real gate. USPS Form 1583 authorises a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency to accept mail for you, and it needs notarising with two forms of ID. As a non-US applicant you do this in an online notary session for $10–35, then the provider takes 1–5 business days. Anytime Mailbox is the cheap marketplace option with 2,500+ locations of uneven quality. Traveling Mailbox is a single operator, so scanning is consistent and included rather than roughly $4 an item.

NOBODY TELLS YOU
A CMRA address is widely rejected as proof of residence. The DMV, landlords and consumer banks opening a personal account all turn it down. It's a business mailing address and a package destination. You'll still need a lease or utility bill for the DMV, and that's a week-three problem.

The IDP is a rental-counter document, not a licence

WHAT THE IDP DOES
Translates
California doesn't require one and recognises your Indian licence outright while you're a non-resident. The IDP carries no driving privilege of its own and extends no deadline. It's worth ₹1,000 for two reasons: rental agencies frequently ask for one, and a police officer can read it.
WHAT ACTUALLY BINDS YOU
10 days
Once you establish residency, meaning a lease or a job, Vehicle Code §12505 gives you ten days to hold a California licence. Not ten days to start the paperwork. California has no reciprocity with India, so you take the written and road tests from scratch.

Get it only if you'll rent a car in month one. If you are:

  • ₹1,000 via Form 4A at the RTO with jurisdiction over the address on your driving licence. An address mismatch is the most common rejection, so file a change of address first if your DL still says your parents' house.
  • Most states also want a Form 1-A medical certificate and many want your air ticket, so you can't do this one very early.
  • Parivahan handles the form and payment, but end-to-end online issuance isn't available in most states. Budget for an RTO visit and 3–7 working days.
  • Valid one year from issue, or until your Indian licence expires, whichever comes first. Treat it as non-renewable from the US.

Health cover for the gap

You will land uninsured. Employer coverage doesn't exist if you're the employer, and Covered California enrolment takes weeks even though moving opens a 60-day special enrolment window.

For the gap, buy a travel medical policy before you board. It runs roughly $1–5 a day for a healthy person in their thirties. Take $100,000 minimum; US emergency care makes lower limits meaningless.

Buy it in India. Policies bought after arrival typically impose a 5–10 day waiting period on illness claims, which defeats the purpose. It's a four-to-eight week bridge: emergency and acute onset only, pre-existing conditions excluded.

Entity, EIN and the bank account

Mercury opens accounts for non-US founders on a passport and an EIN. No SSN, no US visit. It wants a real entity behind it, a live website, and a business description that reads like an operating company. Thin single-member LLCs with no web presence get held or declined.

The long pole is the EIN, not the bank. Without an SSN it's weeks. Start there and Mercury takes days once it's in hand.

The structure decision (C-Corp versus LLC, flip versus fresh entity, ODI and FEMA) is in the entity guide. Don't make that call off a checklist page.

Housing: four weeks, nothing longer

Don't sign a lease from India. Photos lie, the block-to-block variation here is real, and you haven't made the SF-versus-Peninsula call yet. Book a furnished month, whether that's a co-living, a hacker house or a monthly Airbnb, and decide from the ground.
