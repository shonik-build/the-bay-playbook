What these three things actually are

SSN · SOCIAL SECURITY NUMBER
Nine digits, issued to anyone allowed to work here. Invented to track retirement contributions, then drifted into being the closest thing America has to an Aadhaar number. Your employer needs it to pay you, banks want it, and credit cards, phone contracts and the {{ g.dmv.tag }}{{ g.dmv.def }} all key off it. One number, for life. There's a paper card, but the card is almost irrelevant. What matters is that the number exists in the system.
ITIN · TAXPAYER ID NUMBER
Also nine digits, but issued by the {{ g.irs.tag }}{{ g.irs.def }} rather than Social Security. It exists for people who owe US tax but aren't allowed to work here, which in practice means a spouse on a dependent visa. It lets you file a joint return, which usually saves a family real money. It grants no right to work, and you cannot hold both: eligible for an SSN means barred from an ITIN.
THE CALIFORNIA LICENCE
Obvious in function, less obvious in why it's urgent, even if you plan to Uber everywhere. Car insurance here is priced off your local driving record, and having no licence makes it expensive or impossible. A police stop on a foreign licence after you've become a resident is a citation. And it doubles as your everyday photo ID, which is what you want instead of carrying your passport into a bar.

Why the order matters

California gives new residents 10 days to get a state licence. Your SSN takes about three weeks to arrive, and the DMV checks that number electronically with {{ g.ssa.tag }}{{ g.ssa.def }} while you're standing at the counter. The legal deadline and the working sequence contradict each other, and almost everyone breaks the 10-day rule.

Worth knowing that going in. Chasing the 10-day mark is what makes people rush the SSN step and turn up at the DMV before their number is in the system, which fails the appointment. The appointment is the thing to protect. In the Bay Area you may have waited three weeks for it, and it only works if every document is in hand.

Walkthrough A: the SSN

Who qualifies: anyone with permission to work. O-1, H-1B, L-1, E-2 and F-1 with valid work authorisation all clear it. Spouses are where it gets confusing, so the three cases.

L-2 AND E-VISA SPOUSES
Can work, can get an SSN. USCIS has treated them as authorised automatically since November 2021, no separate permit needed. The catch is proof: your I-94 has to show the code L-2S or E-2S, with the S standing for spouse. {{ g.cbp.tag }}{{ g.cbp.def }} has been inconsistent about adding it. Check yours.
H-4 SPOUSES
Need an {{ g.ead.tag }}{{ g.ead.def }} work-permit card first, and only then an SSN. That's its own application and its own wait.
O-3 SPOUSES
Can't work at all, so no SSN. They go to the ITIN route, and they hit the DMV problem further down this page.

Walkthrough B: the California licence

California has no licence-exchange agreement with any country. Not India, not anywhere. The DMV's instructions for a foreign licence holder are the out-of-state process plus a driving test, so you sit both the written and the practical no matter how many years you've been driving in Bangalore.

Your Indian licence plus an International Driving Permit covers you as a visitor. It stops covering you once you're a resident, and an IDP is only a translation of your Indian licence, never a licence in itself.

Start the {{ g.dl44.tag }}{{ g.dl44.def }} application online, book an appointment, and read the California Driver's Handbook. Third-party trackers put the statewide average wait around two weeks, with San Jose and other busy metro offices running 20 to 30 days. Each office page posts its own current estimate, so check two or three before you settle. A 40-minute drive to a quieter office can save a fortnight.

The application stays valid 12 months. A fourth attempt at either test means reapplying and paying again. Driving-test retakes cost $9 each, though the DMV's own licence page still says $7 while the fee schedule updated in July 2026 says $9. Carry a card either way, and note the 2.1% service fee on cards paid in office.

The written test, which the DMV calls the knowledge test, happens at a testing station during the same appointment. No separate booking. The DMV states the passing score is 80% and doesn't publish the question count for adult applicants; test-prep sites consistently report 36 questions with 30 correct, which works out to 83%. Either way you can afford to miss six. Start it before 4:30pm or you'll be sent back another day. It's a real test with a real fail rate, and the people who fail are the ones who assumed twenty years of driving substitutes for reading a handbook.

NOBODY TELLS YOU
Between the two tests you are not licensed to drive alone. Passing the written test gets you a permit, not a licence, and the DMV's FAQ is unambiguous: on a valid foreign licence, an accompanying driver is required to and from the driving test. Plan for someone else to drive you there.

The driving test is a separate appointment, and I'd book it the day you get the permit. It comes in two parts: a pre-drive safety check, where the examiner confirms you can work your own car's indicators, wipers and hazards and that the car is roadworthy, then 20 to 30 minutes of driving with the examiner scoring you. You bring the car, registered and insured to at least California's minimums of $30,000 for injury to one person, $60,000 for more than one, and $15,000 for property damage. A friend's car or a driving school car both work. A rental usually doesn't, not because the DMV bans it but because rental agreements generally don't cover an unlicensed driver taking a test, and the examiner will ask for the registration document.

Walkthrough C: the ITIN, in brief

The money and credit guide carries the full walkthrough. The short version, because it changes what you do in month one: an ITIN is for people who owe US tax but can't get an SSN, which usually means an O-3 or H-4 spouse without work authorisation. If your spouse is eligible for an SSN, they cannot have an ITIN.

You can't apply on a whim. The {{ g.w7.tag }}{{ g.w7.def }} has to travel attached to a tax return unless you meet one of a few narrow exceptions, so the first realistic window is your first US filing, the April after your first year here. The IRS asks for 7 weeks, or 9 to 11 between 15 January and 30 April when everyone files at once. File early in that window rather than on 14 April.

DON'T MAIL YOUR SPOUSE'S PASSPORT
The IRS can hold original documents for up to 60 days. Three in-person routes hand them back at the end of the appointment instead.
TAC · FREE
An IRS Taxpayer Assistance Center with ITIN services. Appointments take several weeks, so book before tax season rather than during it.
VITA · FREE
A {{ g.vita.tag }}{{ g.vita.def }} site that handles ITINs. Only some do, so ask before you turn up.
CAA · PAID
A {{ g.caa.tag }}{{ g.caa.def }}. Plenty of corridor CPA firms are CAAs and will bundle the W-7 with your return, which is convenient and fine. If cost matters, the free options exist and most guides don't mention them.
THE TRAP, AND IT HAS A PHONE NUMBER

If your spouse can't get an SSN

California law says that if you're legally in the US but not eligible for an SSN, you're exempt from the DMV's SSN requirement. The DMV checks that eligibility electronically with SSA while you're in the office. The problem, in the DMV's own words: dependents on visa types like E-1, E-2, L-1 and L-2 may not be eligible for an SSN, but the system response can still read "SSN required", because it doesn't distinguish the main visa holder from the dependent.

When that happens the application hasn't been rejected. It goes to the DMV Issuance Unit in Sacramento for a manual second check of the immigration documents, which takes roughly two weeks. Nobody at the counter will explain this, and the natural reaction is to assume you failed and start over.
