What to do on weekends

A San Francisco library card gets you free entry to more than 50 Bay Area museums and a free vehicle pass to 200-plus California state parks. It costs nothing, takes one visit to a branch, and almost nobody who moves here for work signs up for one in their first year.

The reason people end up doing none of it isn't money or time. It's that the good stuff is buried under parking permit programs, reservation windows that open 90 days out, and seasons nobody mentions until you've missed them.
Weekday events produce contacts. Weekends produce friends.

The standard move here is to meet someone at a Tuesday event and then invite them to a Saturday hike or run, and it is a completely normal invitation to make of someone you've met once. If you take one thing from this page, take that.

Two questions decide almost everything. A lot of month-one arrivals have no US licence and no easy path to a rental, so the car answer matters more here than in most guides.

Founders Running Club is the one to start with. It meets at the William McKinley Monument in the Panhandle at 9:30 every Saturday, with 5K and 10K options, pace groups, coffee afterwards, and pets and friends welcome. It started in San Francisco in July 2022 and now runs in more than 60 cities. It is the single easiest standing plan for someone who has been here three weeks and knows four people.

The free infrastructure nobody uses

Two programmes, both free, both requiring a library card, both largely unknown to people who arrive on a work visa. This is the section to bookmark.

DISCOVER & GO
Library-run museum passes: 50+ museums and cultural attractions across the Bay. Two active reservations at a time. New passes become reservable at 12:01am on the first day of each month, and the calendar shows the current month plus two out. Popular venues go fast.
The eligibility catch is real: you need a full-membership card, not an eCard or a limited-access or student account, and the system checks your zip against the library's service area. Minimum age varies, 14 in Oakland to 18 in Santa Clara County.
STATE LIBRARY PARKS PASS
Your library card checks out a free vehicle day-use pass for 200-plus state parks, valid for one passenger vehicle carrying up to nine people, or one highway-licensed motorcycle. That's the usual $10–20 day-use fee, gone. It now has a permanent $6.75 million ongoing appropriation in the 2026–27 state budget, so it's safe to build a habit around.
Read the limit before you rely on it: not accepted at units operated by federal or local government, or by private concessionaires. That rules out Muir Woods, Point Reyes and Angel Island, which are the three people assume it covers.

On museums, the headline is simpler than most listicles make it. Free Saturdays at the de Young and the Legion of Honor give Bay Area residents free admission every Saturday, and both are free for everyone on the first Tuesday of the month. The Asian Art Museum is free on the first Sunday. Qualifying counties are Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Napa, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Solano and Sonoma, and you'll need a driver's licence or a postmarked envelope as proof. Free admission covers permanent collections only; special exhibitions still cost.

ASIDE · DON'T BOTHER WITH THE FREE-DAY CALENDAR
Chasing monthly free days across a dozen institutions is a hobby, not a strategy. Every-Saturday de Young access plus the library card covers almost everything you'd actually use.

Hikes, and the two that go wrong

The car-free hiking here is genuinely good, which surprises people expecting a car-dependent American metro. Lands End is the right first hike: ocean cliffs, Golden Gate views, no planning, no car.

The Marin Headlands has a bus that exists specifically for weekends and that almost no guide mentions. Muni's 76X runs Saturdays, Sundays and holidays from downtown San Francisco all the way to Fort Cronkhite at Rodeo Beach, roughly hourly. Check the current SFMTA schedule before you commit, because the last bus back has historically been early evening and there is no easy fallback. On weekdays you're on Golden Gate Transit to the Spencer Avenue bus pad instead, where a trailhead starts more or less at the stop, served by lines 101, 132 and 150.

MISSION PEAK · THE PARKING TICKET
This is the hike most Indians here end up doing, and it has the most expensive mistake attached to it. Permits are required on weekends and holidays for the streets around the Stanford Avenue staging area, restricted from Saturday 12:01am through Sunday 11:59pm plus holidays. The staging lot has 43 spaces and fills by 8am. Overflow is 150 spaces on Antelope Drive and Vineyard Avenue. After that it's Ohlone College: 900+ spaces, $4, machines take cash or card, and the route to the summit from there is longer and quieter. Park in the residential streets on a Saturday and you will come back to a ticket.
MUIR WOODS · THE RESERVATION
Reservations for parking and for shuttle seats are required seven days a week, year-round, bookable up to 90 days out at gomuirwoods.com. Parking is $10, entry $15 per adult 16 and over, shuttle $4 round trip with under-16s free. The shuttle runs from the Sausalito Ferry Terminal and Larkspur Landing, which makes it a clean no-car day: ferry, shuttle, redwoods, ferry back. There's no cell service or wifi there, so download the reservation before you go. Weekend slots sell out.

Sport as social infrastructure

The fastest route into a standing weekend group here is a sport, and for this reader that's more likely to be cricket or badminton than pickleball.

Cricket is serious in the Bay in a way that surprises people. The Bay Area Cricket Alliance runs a summer T20 season and a winter T25 across three divisions, on grounds spread over Serramonte, Fischer, MLK, Sierramont, Bay Point and Richmond. The useful mechanic nobody mentions: BACA's own site runs a public board where players post a profile, batting order, bowling style, where they live, contact address, and clubs recruit from it. That's a concrete way in rather than emailing clubs cold. Join before the season, not mid-way, because fields are limited and rosters fill.

Watching is new. The San Francisco Unicorns play Major League Cricket at the Oakland Coliseum, which hosted 12 MLC games in 2026 including the July 18 final, and R. Ashwin signed with them this season after retiring from the IPL in 2025. Tickets started at $15 and the Coliseum is a BART stop, but the season runs June to July, so for most of the year it's a calendar item rather than a weekend plan.

Badminton is the gap in most Bay guides
Bay Badminton Center runs 22 regulation courts across South San Francisco and Milpitas. Bintang has six sites in Burlingame, Campbell, Dublin, Fremont, San Carlos and Santa Clara, and its Fremont location claims 54 badminton courts plus 6 for pickleball. Drop-ins are welcome at all of them. Prices move, so check the site rather than a number in a guide.
Pickleball, if you've never played
Free walk-on play is at Presidio Wall, Louis Sutter in McLaren Park, and Larsen Playground in the Sunset. Presidio Wall has six courts open dawn to dusk, a paddle-queue system, loaner paddles in the storage box, and an introductory class every Tuesday at 10:30am, which is the right entry point. Goldman Tennis Center is the only lit facility, 7am to 10pm, but reservation-only with court fees, and Rec & Park reservations open seven days ahead at 8am and go fast. Parking at Presidio Wall: street parking is allowed south of the rock wall, and street-cleaning tickets there run over $80.

Desi weekend rituals

The honest geography first. Desi density is in the South Bay and East Bay: Fremont, Milpitas, Sunnyvale, Santa Clara. The founder scene is in San Francisco. Most Indian founders end up doing SF on weekdays and one South Bay weekend a month, and the ones who don't build that trip into the calendar tend to stop going entirely by month four.

The classic Saturday or Sunday is temple, then food. All of these have deep Indian food within a few minutes' drive.

Full listings, including Jain centres and the other gurdwaras, sit in the Desi Directory rather than here.

The India Community Center at 525 Los Coches Street in Milpitas is the institutional centre of Bay Area desi life: the largest Indian American community center in North America, a 38,000 square foot facility plus a separate 10,000 square foot table tennis centre. Open seven days a week, explicitly non-religious and non-political, running table tennis, carrom, chess, billiards, Bollywood dance, yoga, language classes, and free legal, medical and career clinics. One thing to check at the desk before you sign up: the Fitness Membership covers gym equipment 8am to 6pm Monday to Friday, which on the face of it doesn't include weekends.

Festivals run year-round and the two that matter are Navratri and Diwali. Diwali falls on Sunday, November 8, 2026, with the five-day sequence starting on Dhanteras on Friday, November 6. Garba season runs the weeks before, and the large Santa Clara Convention Center nights with touring Gujarati artists sell tiered tickets that get meaningfully cheaper if you buy early. The Bay Area Diwali Festival of Lights at Cupertino Memorial Park is free and draws over 13,000 people. For everything else, EventMozo and Sulekha both list dozens of Bay Area Indian events at any given time.

City weekends

Golden Gate Park is the default when you have four hours and no plan. Rent bikes, walk the Botanical Garden, do the de Young on a Saturday when it's free.

The Ferry Building farmers market on Saturday morning is the other institution. Go before 10am, eat down the stalls, and expect to run into people from work. For families, Discover & Go covers a good chunk of the museums kids actually like, which is where the library card starts paying for itself twice over.

Unwinding

The bathhouse and cold-plunge scene here is bigger than in most American cities, partly worth knowing because it's one of the few social settings in SF where nobody is pitching.

Archimedes Banya in Bayview is the loud, social end: saunas, hammam, cold plunge, a rooftop deck over the Bay, chess nights, rooftop yoga, a recurring comedy show for guests in robes. Book carefully. It's co-ed most days but runs separate men's and women's nights, and people turn up on the wrong night and get turned away. Clothing is optional in the water areas, which catches some people out.

Onsen in the Mission is the quiet opposite: a 104-degree communal tub, redwood sauna, steam room, cold plunge, swimsuits required on co-ed days, no phones. Kabuki in Japantown is the Japanese-style option. In the East Bay, Good Hot runs cedar saunas on the shore at around $120 for a six-person sauna, which is $20 a head with friends. Day passes across the city run $45 to $95.

Nightlife, and one legal warning

Bars and clubs here are venues rather than an activity. The social value is in who you go with, and most founder drinking happens at someone's house or after an event rather than at a destination bar. Wine country is the exception, with the standard caveats: book tastings ahead, fees are per person and add up fast, and someone has to drive.

CANNABIS · THE ASYMMETRY
Recreational cannabis is legal under California law and dispensaries are everywhere. It remains a Schedule I controlled substance under federal law, and immigration is federal. The consequence that catches people: a conviction isn't required. An admission of use to a consular officer or to CBP at the border can be enough to support a finding of inadmissibility, and that applies to use that was legal where it happened, including medical use with a state card. Working in the industry carries its own risk. For a non-immigrant visa holder the downside is asymmetric in a way no weekend is worth, because a bad outcome here is your status rather than a fine.
I'm not a lawyer and this page shouldn't be your only source on it. If it's relevant to you, talk to an immigration attorney rather than to the internet.

A note against the thesis of this page

The premise here is that weekends make friendships, and I think that's right. But there's a version of it that goes wrong, which is treating every Saturday as a networking surface. Two things hold up better in practice than the optimising version.

Show up to the same thing repeatedly rather than sampling twelve different things once. The people who end up with actual friends here are the ones at the same run or the same court every week, not the ones with the most varied calendar. Familiarity does the work, and it takes about six weeks.

And some weekends should have no networking value at all. Fly to Tahoe with people you already like, sit in a sauna, drive to Point Reyes and not talk to anyone. The Bay is very good at converting rest into a professional activity, and the people who let it do that tend to be the ones who burn out around month nine.
