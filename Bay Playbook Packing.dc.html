Packing, and the medicines question

What goes in the suitcase, what to leave behind, and which medicines are worth the space. Build your list below, then print it and take it to your doctor.

THE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST
The real cost gap isn't paracetamol. It's the system. Any antibiotic here means a doctor's appointment, $100 to $200 of it without insurance, plus a pharmacy trip, against ₹80 at your chemist at home. That's why a documented 90-day supply of whatever you take daily matters far more than any over-the-counter item you're thinking of packing.

Customs, in five rules

Declared and documented is almost never a problem. Undeclared is. That asymmetry is the whole game.

DO THESE IN INDIA, IN YOUR LAST MONTH
Finish any dental work and get an eye test. Both are expensive and slow here, and neither is covered the way you'd expect. Carry a spare pair of spectacles and your lens prescription, because replacing frames on a US high street costs more than the flight home would suggest.

Clothes for a city with five climates

Summer is the cold, foggy season. This is the trap that catches every July arrival.

Thirteen degrees apart, on the same afternoon, twenty minutes' drive from each other. The local rule is to dress for the coldest neighbourhood you'll pass through, then shed layers as you head east.
THE ONE THING TO BUY BEFORE YOU FLY
A windproof, water-resistant shell with a hood. This is the SF uniform and you will wear it more than anything else you own. Skip the umbrella entirely: the wind here destroys them, and a hood wins every time.
WHAT NOBODY NEEDS
A Delhi-winter parka. It never gets cold enough to justify the suitcase space, and it is the wrong tool anyway: the problem here is wind and damp at 52°F, not dry cold at 4°C.

What not to bring

CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES · REAL SEIZURE RISK
Codeine cough syrups of the Corex type, tramadol, and sleeping pills, unless you have complete paperwork. These carry a hard cap of 50 dosage units and are the one category where getting it wrong costs you more than the medicine. If you genuinely need one of them, that is a conversation with your doctor about documentation, not a packing decision.
